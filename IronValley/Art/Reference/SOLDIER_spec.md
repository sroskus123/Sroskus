# SK_Soldier: infantry soldier, first-person arms, reference and specification

Project: IRON VALLEY (original realistic military FPS prototype, 3 teams x 6, 17 bots in the
browser build).
Assets: **SK_Soldier** (third-person soldier, LOD0 / LOD1 / LOD2, three team variants) and
**FP_Arms** (first-person arms from the same asset), both on the unchanged 57-bone skeleton of
`SK_Human_Base`.

Status: built, textured, validated and exported in Blender only (bpy 4.5.14 LTS, headless, Cycles
CPU). **Nothing was imported into or tested in Unreal** (not available here) and **nothing was
tested in the web game** (not integrated yet). Higgsfield was not used (HF-01 BLOCKED). All numbers
below come from `Art/Previews/Soldier/Soldier_validation.json` and
`Soldier_export_report.json` (regenerated by every run).

## 2. Clothing derived from the body (deforms with the rig)

All garments are built from `SK_Human_Base` faces (`ivsoldier.copy_faces`), so every garment
vertex is a body vertex with the body's **exact weights** (<= 4, normalised) and the body's
**corrective shape keys** copied per vertex (`CS_elbow_*` on the shirt, `CS_knee_*`, `CS_hip_*` on
the trousers). The surface is offset along the normals by a per-region thickness field (smoothed),
then relaxed (Laplacian, boundary pinned) under the constraint "at least 0.8 x the offset outside
the body" so the fabric bridges concavities (armpit, crotch, lumbar) instead of copying them.

| Garment | Region | Offset (mm) | Details |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combat shirt | torso, arms to 5.2 cm proximal of the wrist, neck to a collar line 3 cm high (incl. the nape) | torso 4.5, shoulder 6.5, upper arm 11.5, forearm 10 -> cuff 8.8, collar 8.5 | Cuff **over the glove cuff** (glove cuff reaches 8 cm up the forearm: 2.8 cm overlap); rolled cuff with a 1.6 cm lining towards the elbow; stand-up collar with a clean snapped edge and inner lining; tail tucked 3-5 cm into the trousers (forced >= 8 mm inside at rest) |
| Combat trousers | pelvis + legs, waistband top 1.072 m (lower at the front), hem 15.2 cm | waist 6.5, seat 9.5, thigh 15, knee 12, calf 15 -> hem 18.5 | Worn over the boot shaft (trouser vertices pushed >= 4 mm outside the boots), hem with an inner lip; cargo pockets, knee panels, waistband + belt loops, fly, drawcord hem in the textures |
| Balaclava + gaiter | head + neck above the collar line - 4.5 cm, minus an eye window | 2.6; gaiter 19 (lower face, chin) | The gaiter (neck scarf pulled over the nose) is a thicker, strongly relaxed zone of the same mesh (fills the lips / chin detail); eye window with a rolled edge; faces hidden under the helmet (> 2 cm above the rim) and under the ear cups deleted; decimated to game resolution |
| Face skin | eye window + 1 cm under the edge | 0 | behind the opaque smoked lens; the only skin left |
| Gloves | copies of `SK_Glove_L/R` (hands_gloves.py) | – | decimated 17,884 -> 3,200 tris each, UVs kept (glove square of the clothing atlas), weights re-transferred from the full glove |
| Boots | foot + ankle volume of the body | leather + lining 7.8 (+3 toe box, +2.5 collar) | voxelised (3 mm), morphologically closed (12 mm: toes and arch filled), offset level set, marching cubes, decimated to 1,900 tris; closed shaft top at 20.5 cm (inside the trouser leg); separate rubber soles 3 cm with toe spring, weighted foot -> ball across the flex crease |

**Hidden body parts are deleted.** The shipped soldier contains **no body mesh** except the
259-tri eye patch behind the glasses, so nothing can poke through the clothing. The closed full
body (`SK_Human_Base`, hidden, not exported) is kept only as the volume proxy for the intersection
tests.

**Soldier stands on the boot soles at Z = 0.** The skeleton is unchanged (feet on Z = 0 in the
rest pose); the 3 cm sole overlaps the (deleted) foot's plantar tissue, so the soldier's height is unchanged by the boots; with the helmet the top is at 1.833 m.

**Folds.** At game resolution the hm08 edge length (2-3 cm) cannot carry fold geometry, so the
silhouette gets the loose fit and bulges, and the folds are **baked into the normal map** (height
evaluated per texel on the rest surface, converted with each texel's tangent frame): elbow rings
(stronger on the inner side), bunching above the cuff, armpit diagonals, waist gathers, knee-back
rings, crotch / hip diagonals, stacked folds above the boots, large soft wrinkles, seams (groove +
dashed stitching), ripstop grid and weave. Height range -2.3 / +3.9 mm.

## 3. Gear (rigid, attached to single bones)

Every gear piece is a **closed** mesh. Rigid pieces carry 100 % weight on one bone; the only
blended pieces are fabric bands lying on a garment, which take that garment's weights (and its
corrective shapes, interpolated) so they cannot separate from it.

| Bone | Parts (rigid, 100 %) |
| --- | --- |
| `spine_03` | front and back plate bags (curved R 0.46 m, 26.8 x 33 / 26.8 x 35.5 x 3.4 cm, shooter's cut), placard 23.6 x 13 x 6.8 cm, 3 IV-7 magazines (IV-7 LOD2 mesh cut 1.5 cm below the placard top, tops 4 cm out, brass rounds visible) + bungee cords, admin pouch, zip-on back panel + back pouch, radio pouch + radio + antenna, GP pouch |
| `head` | helmet cover + shell (superellipsoid, high cut, 23.5 x 27.4 cm, top at 1.833 m, 9 mm shell), side rails, NVG shroud + mount, team band, headset cups + arms + boom mic, chin strap, glasses (lens, frame, temple arms) |
| `pelvis` | belt buckle, two rear utility pouches, dump pouch |
| `calf_l`, `calf_r` | hard knee caps (TPR, 13 mm, 5 mm clear of the trousers; held by the trouser knee pocket, no strap) |

| Blended fabric band | Weights from | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cummerbund L / R | shirt (spine_01-03, pelvis, upper arm at the armpit) | the last 2.5 cm at each end forced to `spine_03` (they tuck under the rigid bags) |
| Shoulder straps L / R | shirt (spine_03, clavicle, neck; small upper-arm / head share near the collar) | ends 1.5 cm forced to `spine_03` |
| Belt | trousers (pelvis, spine_01, thigh) + the trousers' hip correctives | 5 mm clear of the waistband, 4.8 cm tall (3.6 cm at the front) |

Placement is measured, not guessed: bags slide towards the body until their inner side is 6.5 mm
off the shirt; bands are ray-cast onto the garment (back-face hits rejected) and offset along the
smoothed normal; knee caps are pushed out along their attachment normal until no garment vertex is
inside them (inside confirmed by ray parity) and they are 5 mm off the trousers. Pouches mounted
on another gear piece (placard / admin pouch on the front bag, back panel on the back bag, back
pouch on the panel, radio / GP pouches on the cummerbund side of the bags, utility / dump pouches
on the belt) are slid onto their carrier until the gap is 0.6 mm (`snap_to`, signed distance), and
belt pouches then swing about their top edge (like a MOLLE pouch hanging from the belt) until they
clear the trousers by 3 mm (`tilt_clear`: 7-14 deg). The attachment gaps are measured in
`Soldier_validation.json -> gear_attachment_gap_mm`.

## 4. Materials and textures

Four materials on the soldier (4 draw calls per bot), three on FP_Arms. Metal / roughness,
BaseColor sRGB, ORM linear (R AO, G roughness, B metallic), Normal OpenGL + `_Normal_DX` for Unreal.
All textures are synthesised per texel in numpy on the rest positions (`soldier_tex.py`): patterns
are continuous across UV seams; AO = Cycles AO bake (2 cm) x seam cavity.

| Material | Textures (`Art/Textures/Characters/Soldier/`) | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `M_Soldier_Cloth_<Team>` | `T_Soldier_Cloth_<Team>_BaseColor.png` **1024** (per team) + shared `T_Soldier_Cloth_Normal(_DX).png`, `_ORM.png` **2048** | shirt, trousers, balaclava + gaiter, face patch, helmet cover, gloves (glove textures resampled into a 512 px square) |
| `M_Soldier_GearFabric_<Team>` | shared `T_Soldier_Gear_BaseColor / Normal(_DX) / ORM.png` **2048**, x **baseColorFactor** = team gear tint | plate carrier, pouches, belt (authored neutral grey 0.62) |
| `M_Soldier_GearHard` | same gear textures, untinted | helmet shell, rails, shroud, headset, glasses, magazines, radio, antenna, bungees, buckle, knee caps, boots, soles |
| `M_Soldier_Team_<Team>` | `T_Soldier_Team_<Team>_BaseColor.png` **256** + shared `_Normal`, `_ORM` 256 | armbands, helmet band |
| `M_FPArms_Sleeve_<Team>` | `T_Soldier_FPArms_<Team>_BaseColor.png` + shared `_Normal(_DX)`, `_ORM` **2048** | FP sleeves |
| `M_FPArms_Glove` | `Art/Textures/Characters/Gloves/T_Glove_Black_*`, `T_Glove_Normal(_DX)` 2048 | FP gloves |

Texel density: shirt / trousers 9.4 px/cm, balaclava 12.3, helmet cover 11.8, gloves (3P) 13.6,
FP sleeves **30.4 px/cm**, FP gloves **54.5 px/cm**. Atlas coverage: clothing 44 % (+ glove square),
gear 43 %, FP 63 % (P2: the clothing / gear packing could be tighter).

**Team variants = small texture swap + material parameters:** a bot of another team swaps the
1024 clothing BaseColor and the 256 team-band texture and changes the gear material's base colour
factor; Normal / ORM and the gear textures are shared. In glTF the tint is the material's
`baseColorFactor` (checked in the exported GLBs, section 11); in Unreal it becomes a material
instance parameter (the FBX only carries the texture).

## 7. First-person arms (FP_Arms)

- Mesh: the shirt sleeves from 32 % down the upper arm to the cuff (with the cuff lining), the
  armbands inside that range, a closing cap at the cut (inner-sleeve faces, never meant to be
  seen), and the gloves decimated from the full-resolution gloves to 5,200 tris each. **12,491
  triangles**, 3 materials, 8 elbow corrective shape keys.
- Skeleton: the same 57-bone skeleton (animations are shared with the body); weights only on
  the arm subset (clavicle, upper arm + twist, forearm + twist, hand, fingers; `spine_03` a few %
  at the cut).
- Texture density: sleeves 30.4 px/cm (3.2 x the third-person soldier), gloves 54.5 px/cm (the
  original glove textures).
- Cameras used for the evidence (80 deg horizontal FOV, 1600 x 900, near clip 1 cm): **hip** =
  the eye (head bone x rest eye point (0, -0.131, 1.687)); **ADS** = on the optic axis at the
  cheek-weld eye point 14 cm behind `socket_ads` (the web game puts its ADS camera at
  `socket_ads`, D11 -- closer to the optic, so less of the arms is visible there). Looking up / down
  = the whole upper body pitched (spine 55 %, neck / head the rest), the rifle re-placed and both arms
  re-solved (not a camera-only rotation).
- The cut ring was projected into every view: **0 of 46 cut vertices inside the frustum** in all
  six views (`_fp_views.json`). Renders: `Soldier_fp_{hip,ads}_{level,up,down}.png`. The iron
  sights are folded (-90 deg) as in the game when the optic is used.

## 8. Holding the IV-7 (stances used for the checks and renders)

- The IV-7 (LOD0, read-only from `iv7_carbine.blend`) is placed from the view direction; the
  firing hand uses `rifle_grip_index_straight`, the support hand `support_handguard`
  (Hand_Poses.json `hand_attach` in the socket frames); both arms by analytic two-bone IK
  (`ivhands.arm_ik`, twist bones driven), fingers from the same poses.
- **ADS:** a least-squares posture solve (spine twist / lean, clavicle elevation / protraction,
  neck flexion / lateral bend, head roll, butt height, support-hand position along the
  handguard) puts the butt pad on the right shoulder pocket and the cheek towards the stock; then
  the rifle slides forward until the stock no longer sinks into the shoulder (<= 1.5 mm), the head
  tilts away from the stock until the headset cup, glasses and gaiter clear it, and (aiming up) the
  rifle moves out from the head. The support hand grips the handguard 7.5 cm behind
  `socket_support_hand` (same handguard section; the fitted grip is unchanged).
- **Hip / ready:** rifle shouldered with the butt 3.5 cm up the pocket, pitched 2 deg down, head up.
- Measured: rifle vs body / gear / garments **0 penetration > 1 mm** in all four rifle poses; gloves
  on the grip / handguard <= 1.6 mm (contact); support-hand IK reach error 0-12 mm.
- Limitation: the third-person ADS pose keeps the eye about 7 cm from the optic axis (the chosen
  posture limits cannot bring a 1.80 m body's eye onto a 7 cm-high optic line with the butt on the
  shoulder); it reads as aiming in third person, and the first-person view uses the sight line
  directly.

## 9. Validation method (`soldier.py --stages validate`)

Poses: bind (A-pose), arms raised (clavicle 22 + abduction 105 deg), deep squat (hip 100 / knee 130
/ ankle 30, knees apart, arms forward), sprint stride (front hip 65 / knee 95, rear hip -22 / knee
35, arms swinging), aim ADS level / +35 / -35 and hip-ready with the IV-7. Twist bones and all
corrective shapes are driven exactly as the runtime rule says.

- **Penetration** of a vertex into a closed mesh: nearest-face sign as a prefilter, confirmed by
  ray parity (3 rays); depth = distance to the other surface; tolerance 1 mm.
  Sets: gear in the (closed) body proxy, body proxy in each gear piece, each garment (shirt,
  trousers, balaclava, boots, soles, gloves, armbands) in each gear piece, rifle in body / gear and
  garments / gear in each rifle part.
- **Layer order** (poke-through between clothing layers): inner-layer vertices in front of the
  outer garment's shell (its linings / rolled edges excluded): shirt tail vs trousers, balaclava vs
  collar (the vertices inside the collar at rest), boot shaft vs trousers, glove cuffs vs sleeves.
- **Weights:** every part: max influences, unweighted vertices, sums; rigid parts: exactly one
  bone at 1.0.

## 12. Engine notes (unverified in Unreal / not yet integrated in the web game)

- **Skeleton:** import `SK_Soldier.fbx` onto the existing `SKEL_Human_Base` (same 57 bones, rest pose
  and axes; FBX settings `ivlib.FBX_UNREAL_OPTS`, cm bake). `FP_Arms.fbx` uses the same skeleton.
- **Runtime rules** (same as SK_Human_Base, `Soldier.runtime.json`): drive the twist bones and the
  corrective morph targets by joint angle every frame. LOD0 carries 24 correctives (elbow, knee,
  hip; also on the cummerbund / straps / belt where they follow the garment), FP_Arms 8 (elbow);
  LOD1 / LOD2 none. Without the rule the mesh deforms as plain LBS.
- **Team variant:** swap the clothing BaseColor (1024), the team-band BaseColor (256) and the gear
  fabric tint (glTF `baseColorFactor`; UE material instance parameter). The glTF exports carry the
  tint per team file; the FBX has the Alfa materials.
- **Materials:** use `_Normal_DX` in Unreal (the FBX already references it), the OpenGL maps in
  three.js; ORM is linear. The lens is opaque (dark glossy, no transparency sorting).
- **FP / 3P visibility:** render FP_Arms only in the first-person camera and SK_Soldier only for
  others (and for the player's shadow); the FP sleeve cut is capped but was never in view.
- **LOD switch distances** (suggestion, not measured in engine): LOD0 < 12 m, LOD1 12-30 m, LOD2
  beyond.
- **Web budget (17 bots):** e.g. 2 bots at LOD0, 5 at LOD1, 10 at LOD2 = 2 x 28.2k + 5 x 11.3k + 10 x
  4.6k = 159k triangles, 4 draw calls per bot. NOT TESTED in the browser.
